A1 From Evaluation to Evolution: Benchmarking Image Editing and Evolving Image Models

arXiv: 2610.41210 QwenRSIAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Real-world image editing requires coordinating multiple visual transformations with content preservation and reference constraints, yet aggregate scores can readily obscure unmet local requirements. 真实世界的图像编辑需要协调多个视觉转换与内容保存和参考约束，但总分数很容易模糊未满足的本地要求。

Effective evaluation must therefore identify local failures and translate them into priorities for subsequent model improvement. 因此，有效的评估必须确定当地的失败，并将其转化为后续模型改进的优先事项。

We introduce WeGenEditBench, comprising 2,740 evaluation instances and 148 task types across single-image, text-intensive, and multi-reference editing. 我们引入了WeGenEditBench ，包括2,740个评估实例和148种任务类型，涵盖单图像、文本密集型和多引用编辑。

Pre-generated checklists shared across models decompose each request into atomic requirements; item-level deductions and visual evidence are established first and then summarized into scores for edit execution, preservation, visual quality, and task-specific correctness. 跨模型共享的预生成清单将每个请求分解为原子要求；首先建立项目级扣除和视觉证据，然后总结为编辑执行、保存、视觉质量和特定任务正确性的分数。

To support the experience-acquisition component of recursive self-improvement (RSI), we develop WeGenEvoStudio, a unified multi-agent harness for RSI-oriented learning that integrates model-conditioned failure diagnosis, retrieval-grounded data construction, supervised adaptation, and re-evaluation. 为了支持递归自我改进（ RSI ）的经验获取组件，我们开发了WeGenEvoStudio ，这是一种面向RSI学习的统一多Agent线束，集成了模型条件故障诊断、检索接地数据构建、监督适应和重新评估。

Among 15 evaluated configurations, GPT-Image-2 leads single-image editing with an Overall score of 4.076, while Seedream-5.0-Pro leads text-intensive editing at 4.291. 在15个评估的配置中， GPT-Image-2以4.076的总分领先于单幅图像编辑，而Seedream-5.0-Pro以4.291领先于文本密集型编辑。

Evolution experiments across four generation and editing benchmarks demonstrate substantial targeted gains: on their report-selected Top-5 single-image targets, FLUX.2 [dev] improves Overall from 1.083 to 3.517, while Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 gains 13.2%. 跨四个生成和编辑基准的进化实验显示了显著的目标收益：在其报告选择的前五个单一图像目标上， FLUX.2 [dev]将整体从1.083提高到3.517 ，而Qwen-Image-Edit-2511提高了13.2%。

Together, our benchmark and harness provide a foundation for developing more reliable image models through interpretable evaluation and feedback-driven learning. 我们的基准和利用共同为通过可解释的评估和反馈驱动的学习开发更可靠的图像模型奠定了基础。

A2 TalentRL: Strategy-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Hierarchical Skill Routing

arXiv: 2610.41143 MetaQwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Natural-language skills enable large language model (LLM) agents to reuse procedures across tasks, yet most systems retrieve individual skills from a flat SkillBank without explicitly modeling the high-level strategy that governs their selection and use.

We introduce TalentRL, a strategy-guided reinforcement learning framework for hierarchical routing over procedural memory.

Coarse-grained talents represent recurring strategies for skill use, while fine-grained skills provide executable procedures.

For each task, the policy model selects a talent that guides execution and softly prioritizes its associated skills during similarity-based retrieval.

However, since task outcomes depend jointly on talent selection, retrieved skill context, and downstream execution, terminal rewards alone cannot isolate the contribution of the routing decision.

TalentRL therefore trains talent routing with reward contrasts between rollouts of the same task under matched interventions on the talent-conditioned context, and optimizes downstream execution with group-relative advantages.

Across seven open-domain and multi-hop question-answering benchmarks, talent-aware supervised fine-tuning improves average success rate by 7.53%, 6.71%, and 9.21% over the corresponding zero-shot baselines for Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-8B, and Llama-3.1-8B, respectively.

Reinforcement learning further improves these SFT checkpoints by 7.58% to 9.32%, and TalentRL achieves the highest average success rate among all compared methods on all three backbones.

On the agentic benchmarks ALFWorld and WebShop, TalentRL achieves the highest ALFWorld success rate (99.3%) and the highest WebShop score (90.7) among all compared methods.

A3 Counterfactual Pairwise Augmentation Improves Small Pointwise LLM Judges

arXiv: 2610.41149 GoogleQwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Agentic systems increasingly gate individual steps with a pointwise judge that scores a response against a criterion.

Small language models are attractive for this role because they can be served and specialized efficiently.

Such a judge must locate the criterion's decision boundary, but pointwise labels under-determine that boundary in fine-tuning: labeled responses to different queries differ along many dimensions at once, so they do not reveal which features decide the label.

We introduce CPA (Counterfactual Pairwise Augmentation), which makes the deciding difference explicit in training.

For each pointwise item, CPA synthesizes and audits a counterfactual response to the same query intended to carry the opposite label.

It frames the accepted pair as a pairwise task asking which of the two responses satisfies the criterion.

The judge is fine-tuned jointly on both pointwise and pairwise datasets and deployed in pointwise mode only.

A second-order analysis shows when this should outperform the prevailing strategy of merging separately trained pointwise and pairwise experts: merging moves every parameter direction toward the pairwise expert by the same fraction, whereas joint training can hold the pointwise optimum wherever the pairwise loss is flat and absorb its curvature where it is sharp.

This is the regime that CPA's same-source, counterfactually minimal pairwise view is designed to produce.

On separate Qwen3-8B LoRA adapters for six binary criteria, CPA improves criterion-averaged pointwise macro-F1 by 2.5 points over pointwise-only training and outperforms model merging on all six criteria at every tested merge weight.

It also surpasses the strongest zero-shot frontier judge we evaluate, Gemini 3.6 Flash, by 11.8 points.

A4 Verify--Backtrack Loop: Structured Precondition Recovery for Long-Horizon Language Agents

arXiv: 2610.41564 OpenAIDeepSeekQwen

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Language agents increasingly tackle long-horizon tasks in interactive environments, where actions often depend on latent preconditions.

The core difficulty is not trajectory length but recovery: when a prerequisite is missing or an earlier decomposition fails, trying another plausible action rarely helps.

ReAct and later reflection or adaptive-planning methods support local correction, but express recovery as free-form text, with no explicit control state linking the terminal goal, the active goal, and the failure source.

We introduce the Verify--Backtrack Loop (VBL), a training-free extension of ReAct that turns precondition discovery and failure diagnosis into structured planning-state updates.

VBL maintains the task decomposition as a bounded goal stack and, on goal activation, execution failure, or material state change, jointly verifies two horizons---whether the active goal contributes to the terminal goal and whether it is feasible in the current state.

These dual-horizon judgments, combined with typed failure attribution, trigger explicit operations---Push, Pop, retry, or BacktrackTo the parent.

We evaluate VBL with Qwen3.5-9B, GPT-4o, and DeepSeek-V3.2 on AppWorld, ALFWorld, and ScienceWorld.

VBL improves over ReAct and reflection-based control on every backbone--benchmark pair.

On the challenging AppWorld setting, it improves task success by up to 22.07 percentage points over ReAct.

Ablations show that removing either verification or diagnosis degrades performance, in several settings below ReAct, confirming that the two mechanisms are complementary.

These results show that reliable long-horizon interaction benefits from revising the planning state, not merely the next action.

A5 Latent Sparse Attention: Towards Subquadratic Ultra-Long Context Attention

arXiv: 2610.42147 QwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Agentic workflows are increasingly shifting towards long-horizon tasks, such as open-ended scientific work, where models benefit from long context windows.

However, at ultra-long context (above 1M tokens), full attention is prohibitively expensive.

A promising approach is sparse attention, in which a dedicated indexer selects a subset of keys for the attention.

Yet current methods of indexing either still have quadratic cost in context size or lose accuracy at ultra-long context scale.

We address this with Latent Sparse Attention (LSA), an efficient attention method that maintains accuracy at ultra-long context.

In LSA, keys are hierarchically clustered in a learned latent space, and queries score the hierarchy of clusters instead of scoring every key, making prefill, decode and training faster at long context.

We propose two variants, Distilled-LSA (D-LSA), which can be applied to pre-trained models via distillation, and Native-LSA (N-LSA), which is trained natively on the next-token loss alone.

On GLM-5.3-FLASH (QWEN-3.8-27B) at 10M tokens, D-LSA speeds up one attention layer by 22.7x (72.4x) in prefill and 4.6x (42.5x) in decode over its original sparse attention (full attention), while keeping similar accuracy across a variety of tasks.

N-LSA matches the accuracy of full attention with a training speedup of 1.6x per layer at 128k tokens.

LSA offers practical and efficient attention for models to benefit from ultra-long context.

A6 SecPO: Principled Adversarial Training for Prompt Injection Security

arXiv: 2610.41998 QwenAgentLLM

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Recognized as a top threat to AI agents, prompt injection allows instructions from untrusted data (email, documents, websites) to manipulate the agent's operation.

The existing best-defended LLM, Meta-SecAlign, is trained with the DPO loss but still suffers from attack success rates (ASRs) approaching 100% under adaptive prompt injections.

One reason is that DPO anchors the model around the undefended reference model, which is insecure.

We show an idealized reference model---an injection-invariant, utility-preserving oracle---can nevertheless be approximated, as its output distribution in training is interestingly accessible.

This new observation motivates a theoretically principled SecPO loss.

Another Meta-SecAlign shortcoming is that it trains on weak simulated attacks.

We implement an efficient adversarial training (AT) pipeline that synthesizes strong injected inputs (best@240), and train the model on them.

Our AT reduces ASRs by 1.5-14.5$\times$ across three SoTA adaptive attacks, compared with Meta-SecAlign; the SecPO loss reduces them by a further 2.4-4.6$\times$ compared with using DPO loss.

In agentic workflows, our SecPO + AT Qwen3.6-27B is much more secure than Meta-SecAlign: 8.8% vs.

78.8% AgentDojo ASR@800 under the AttackerMovesSecond attack.

Moreover, our defended model shows no noticeable reduction in benign utility in nine benchmarks covering agentic tool calling, instruction following, science, and math.

A7 Can Agentic Code Review Catch Vulnerabilities Before They Are Merged?

arXiv: 2610.33509 OpenAIAnthropicAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

LLM agents have been increasingly adopted for security-focused code review, but existing benchmarks mostly derive the reviewed changes from their later fixes instead of the real-world bug-introducing commits.

In this paper, we present SecPRBench, the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating agentic security review on validated real-world code changes.

More specifically, we have built an automated agentic pipeline to autonomously harvest, build, and validate massive real-world bug-introducing commits for popular software systems.

To ensure precise evaluation, we also adopt LLM-as-a-judge to credit the findings matching ground-truth vulnerabilities.

To date, we have collected 265 tasks from nine critical systems, including Firefox, V8, and OpenSSL with millions of lines of code.

Across eight frontier models, recall ranges from 32.5% to 78.1%, with Grok 4.6 and GPT-6 sol ranking at the top.

Our study also shows that most misses are not simply oversights: in 68% of them, the agent reports on a file that holds the flaw but argues for a different mechanism.

Security-focused harnesses also behave differently: OpenAI's Codex Security lowers recall by 8.3 points, mostly by abstaining, while Anthropic's Defending Code harness raises it by 7.7 points, at a higher cost.

A8 Towards Understanding Score-Seeking in LLMs

arXiv: 2610.41014 OpenAIAgentLLM

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Production large language models sometimes reward-hack, exploiting flaws in how tasks are scored rather than performing the tasks as intended, sometimes in highly sophisticated and adversarial ways.

We investigate the motivations driving this behavior in GPT 5.6 Sol, GPT 5.5, and a checkpoint of OpenAI o3 without safety training ("exp-rl-cap-late") by observing model behavior in agentic environments where the grader is leaked.

In our environments, the models appear to be score-seeking: trying to score well according to graders that they believe assess their current actions, even at the expense of violating explicit user instructions.

They appear to care about score itself rather than its downstream consequences, violating instructions at approximately the same rate whether the score is indicated to be used for training, for evaluation, or not at all.

We observe evidence that the more recent models care to some extent about scores that do not assess their own current actions.

GPT 5.6 Sol and GPT 5.5 help other agents 24.6 percentage points more often on average when the other agent stands to gain a score of 1 rather than 0 from being helped.

However, when grading or monitoring another trajectory, we find models never collude by distorting its score.

A9 TSFLab: A Self-Improving Agent-Agnostic Workbench for Time Series Forecasting Research

arXiv: 2610.40901 AnthropicRSIAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

As publications and products on time series forecasting (TSF) proliferate, researchers face a growing burden of analyzing existing work.

First, not all publications release code.

For instance, only about 20% of arXiv TSF papers have a code link in the surveyed period.

Second, released code also requires manual maintenance.

Thus, TSF researchers must constantly adapt or implement existing works.

However, independent implementations can differ in experimental settings and are difficult to maintain.

We therefore propose to use AI agents for benchmarking TSF research and even supporting autoresearch, e.g., developing new TSF models.

Yet directly applying existing coding agents, such as Claude Code and Codex, is suboptimal: prohibitive costs, limited scalability to large research projects (e.g., benchmarking), and lack of self-maintenance.

To address these challenges, we propose TSFLab, a self-improving agent-agnostic "workbench" that equips compatible coding agents with shared resources and tools for low-cost TSF research and continuous maintenance of research resources.

Our experiments cover reproduction, benchmarking, and new method development.

In reproduction and benchmarking, TSFLab improves task completion by 20-163% and cuts fresh tokens per completed task by 11-66% across baselines.

A pilot study of agent-driven method development also shows improved forecasting accuracy via TSFLab, with 32.8-73.6% fewer fresh tokens and 9.2-68.8% less development time than the other settings.

A10 LanG 2.0: Energy-Efficient Agentic AI for Semi-Automated Security Operations at Scale

arXiv: 2610.41072 AnthropicAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Security operations centers (SOCs) need to turn terabytes of alerts, logs and flows into trusted, affordable decisions to unveil hidden threats.

We present LanG 2.0, an agentic system that detects threats, correlates evidence and writes rules, investigating whether small local models can replace frontier models affordably and safely when structural safeguards, not model accuracy, provide the guarantees.

It incorporates entity-anchored correlation, human approval of rule changes and a tamper-evident ledger.

Tests span eight flow datasets from five networks, real XDR alerts and multistage campaigns.

Against its predecessor, campaign correlation precision rises from 18.75% to 51.81%, and rule acceptance from at most 60.00% to 98.33%.

Against Claude Opus 5.5, scoring flows against each network's normal traffic detects attacks on unseen networks at 73.42% F1 without false positives, versus 58.10% with 49.00% false positives, for at least 650 times less energy.

Finetuned models statistically match it on alert classification and rule writing for 5.6 to 59 times less energy.

The work demonstrates LanG's practical scalability, processing 3.33 million records in 6.4 minutes, over 30 million hourly, and its safeguards resisted prompt injection, label poisoning and ledger tampering, so small local models can carry most SOC detection affordably and safely.
