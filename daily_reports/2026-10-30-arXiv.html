A1 Gen-Searcher: Reinforcing Agentic Search for Image Generation

arXiv: 2603.28767 QwenAgentic RLAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Recent image generation models have shown strong capabilities in generating high-fidelity and photorealistic images.

However, they are fundamentally constrained by frozen internal knowledge, thus often failing on real-world scenarios that are knowledge-intensive or require up-to-date information.

In this paper, we present Gen-Searcher, as the first attempt to train a search-augmented image generation agent, which performs multi-hop reasoning and search to collect the textual knowledge and reference images needed for grounded generation.

To achieve this, we construct a tailored data pipeline and curate two high-quality datasets, Gen-Searcher-SFT-10k and Gen-Searcher-RL-6k, containing diverse search-intensive prompts and corresponding ground-truth synthesis images.

We further introduce KnowGen, a comprehensive benchmark that explicitly requires search-grounded external knowledge for image generation and evaluates models from multiple dimensions.

Based on these resources, we train Gen-Searcher with SFT followed by agentic reinforcement learning with dual reward feedback, which combines text-based and image-based rewards to provide more stable and informative learning signals for GRPO training.

Experiments show that Gen-Searcher brings substantial gains, improving Qwen-Image by around 16 points on KnowGen and 15 points on WISE.

We hope this work can serve as an open foundation for search agents in image generation, and we fully open-source our data, models, and code.

A2 From Legacy Fortran to Portable Kokkos: An Autonomous Agentic AI Workflow

arXiv: 2509.12443 OpenAIQwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Scientific applications still rely heavily on legacy Fortran codebases built for homogeneous, CPU-based systems.

As High-Performance Computing (HPC) shifts toward GPU-accelerated architectures, many accelerators lack native Fortran bindings, creating a need to translate and optimize legacy code for portability.

Kokkos offers performance portability through a single-source C++ abstraction, but manual porting from Fortran demands significant expertise and time.

While large language models (LLMs) show promise in source-to-source code generation, their use in autonomous workflows for translating and optimizing parallel code is largely unexplored.

This paper presents an agentic AI workflow in which specialized LLM agents collaborate to translate, validate, compile, run, test, debug, and optimize Fortran kernels into portable Kokkos C++ programs without human intervention, bounded by configurable retry and optimization thresholds.

We evaluate the pipeline over five runs per configuration on an NVIDIA A100, characterizing its variability in success rates, token cost, and optimization behavior, on benchmark kernels including a real-world multi-file kernel from a production climate model.

Proprietary models such as GPT-5 and o4-mini-high completed the full workflow most reliably, producing optimized code that exceeded the Fortran baselines for only a few U.S.

dollars, whereas open-weights models such as gpt-oss-120b and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B handled simpler kernels but struggled with complex Kokkos abstractions such as parallel reductions and hierarchical parallelism.

This work demonstrates, at the kernel level, the feasibility of agentic AI for Fortran-to-Kokkos transformation and, more broadly, for structured software-engineering tasks such as automated code modernization and refactoring.

A3 One to More, More to One: Category-Aware Iterative Expert Training for Software Engineering Agents

arXiv: 2609.23377 QwenAgentic RLAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Repository-level software engineering (SWE) comprises heterogeneous task categories, whose progress under pooled agentic reinforcement learning can be uneven: gains in some categories coincide with regressions in others, while aggregate resolution obscures these changes.

Motivated by this category see-saw, we develop a category-aware expert-training and policy-integration framework.

Executable task construction and SWE Labeler, an evidence-grounded multi-axis labeling system, organize the training pools.

Initial category-specific RL improves average training success while leaving uneven instance-level progress, motivating explicit consolidation of successful behavior and policy-adaptive task selection.

Same-origin category experts alternate long-horizon Agentic-miniRL with Refresh-Repair-Expand (RRE): the updated policy refreshes instance mastery, reuses its own verified successful trajectories for Repair SFT, and reselects tasks for further RL.

Label-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates the experts into one deployable student, with ReLU-gated reward extrapolation keeping only each teacher's improving direction over the reference.

Expert training and policy integration require no external model to provide solution trajectories or action targets.

On a fixed, audit-filtered Pro-618 subset, we connect Pooled RL and Balanced RL, expert development, and single-model integration through overall and per-category resolution and expert-gain recovery.

In final-model evaluations, MOPD achieves mean resolution of 86.19% on SWE-bench Pro V2 and 71.78% on SWE-bench Multilingual, exceeding the base model by 4.57 and 3.89 percentage points, respectively.

The same three 27B experts also improve a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B student across the evaluated task sets.

A4 SCOPE: Selective Critical-Step On-Policy Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv: 2610.38391 QwenAgentic RLAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Outcome-based reinforcement learning provides coarse supervision for long-horizon LLM agents: failed trajectories may contain correct steps, while successful trajectories can contain errors.

Trajectory-level skills can also shift teacher preferences at unrelated steps, introducing off-target distillation signals.

We introduce SCOPE, a framework for Selective Critical-Step On-Policy Self-Distillation.

After a supervised warm-up, the policy analyzes its own trajectories, generates step-specific hindsight skills, and distills their guidance only at selected error steps.

A bounded signed teacher-student coefficient stabilizes auxiliary updates while preserving positive reinforcement and negative correction.

A success-relative pairwise length reward further encourages efficient task completion.

Experiments on ALFWorld and WebShop demonstrate consistent improvements across multiple model backbones.

With Qwen2.5-3B, SCOPE achieves 97.7% success on ALFWorld and 85.2% on WebShop, outperforming SEED by 5.9 and 6.3 percentage points, respectively.

On unseen ALFWorld tasks, SCOPE reaches 89.6% success, improving over GRPO by 23.2 percentage points with 46.7% fewer interactions.

Scaling to Qwen3-32B, SCOPE achieves 99.2% success on seen ALFWorld tasks and 94.0% on unseen tasks.

A5 TreasuryQA Bench: Can AI Agents Work an Archive of Scanned Financial Records?

arXiv: 2610.38924 OpenAIAnthropicAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Organisations keep decades of financial records as scanned PDFs on shared drives, and AI agents can now reach those drives through Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers.

Existing benchmarks do not mirror this production setting because they simplify the task.

In some, the relevant documents are handed to the agent, so it never has to search.

In others, the agent searches through documents with pre-extracted text, so it never has to read scanned images.

We introduce TreasuryQA Bench, which requires both: 70 questions over 1,435 scanned U.S.

Treasury publications, held in Veris's high-fidelity Dropbox twin.

In each task the agent searches for the relevant file through Dropbox's MCP server, reads the pages it needs with Reducto's hosted OCR server or its own tools, and replies with one exact figure.

A Python grader checks each answer against an accepted range around the correct value.

In this paper we present results from ten agents.

The best, Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, solves 64.8% of attempts and the worst, GPT-5.6 Terra in Codex, solves 16.7%, so the benchmark is far from saturated.

We also test how scan quality affects performance: every agent solves fewer tasks on the blurriest scans.

Most failed attempts reply with a wrong number rather than no number, and the most common cause is a digit misread from the scan.

Three worked examples are included with their answer keys.

A6 Pre-Crime for Agents, at the Last Second: Probing When a Recruited Agent Gives In

arXiv: 2610.40588 QwenAgentMulti-Agent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

When AI agents share channels and credentials, one agent can press another to break its operator's rule, a first step towards collusion.

Output filters check only what an agent has already written.

Can an overseer tell beforehand that an agent is about to give in?

We show that a linear probe on the agent's activations can, in a controlled multi-agent setting.

Across six scenarios, a peer presses a target for up to 20 rounds to reveal a secret, such as a deploy token; the target often refuses, then gives in.

Read at each model call before its output is written, the probe picks out the one that leaks.

On held-out Qwen3-32B episodes, at a threshold set to flag 5% of benign calls, it catches 82% of leaking calls.

At a similar flag rate, a zero-shot decision-model monitor catches 26%.

On gpt-oss-20b, a probe on its reasoning reaches AUROC 0.91.

A bag-of-words classifier trained on the same calls comes close, so most of the gain comes from training on labels, not activations.

The probe also flags permitted disclosures: it reads the coming disclosure more than the violation.

The warning is short-range, and halting on any alarm would stop most benign episodes, which a second text check does not fix.

A warning raised before generation could let an overseer stop an agent that is about to give in, ahead of others acting on its messages or recruitment spreading through a swarm.

A7 Vendster: An Evaluation Arena to Investigate How Forecasting Tool Quality Affects LLM Agent Behaviors in Buyer-Vendor Negotiation

arXiv: 2610.38292 OpenAIAnthropicDeepSeek

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Time-series forecasters are ranked almost entirely by accuracy metrics, but a forecast's decision value, its effect on the downstream decision, need not track accuracy and is rarely measured.

This problem compounds as large language model (LLM) agents increasingly consume forecasters as tools, mediating them through stochastic, opaque policies.

We present Vendster, an evaluation arena measuring how a forecaster's bias and prediction-interval width change the outcome of a buyer--vendor discount negotiation between two LLM agents.

In the reference instance Vendster-M5, sampling base demand patterns from the M5 dataset, we evaluate two frontier-closed LLM families (Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.6 Sol) and two open ones (DeepSeek-V3.2 and Qwen3-235B-A22B).

Model-generated forecasts raise sales target attainment by up to +17 percentage points (p<0.001), yet standard forecast evaluation metrics such as relWQL and MASE do not predict negotiation outcomes at a statistically significant level.

To isolate the causal structure, we substitute in synthetic forecasts with controlled bias and interval width.

Under-prediction bias significantly improves outcomes for agents that react more to the forecasts---dose-response slopes -0.387 and -0.416 (p<0.001).

Interval width has no statistically significant effect at zero bias.

These findings indicate that the direction of forecast bias and agent forecast-reactivity are more strongly associated with downstream outcomes than forecast accuracy.

Vendster is fully customizable and released under CC BY-NC 4.0 at https://github.com/amazon-science/Vendster.

A8 Compact-Bench: Measuring What Context Compaction Costs Long-Horizon Coding Agents

arXiv: 2610.31710 AnthropicDeepSeekKimi

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Context compaction lets long-horizon coding agents continue beyond their context windows, but can erase the state needed to finish a task.

Prior evaluations measure efficiency, retained information, or end-to-end success, leaving the effect of a specific history replacement difficult to isolate.

Compact-Bench pairs full-history and summary-only continuations from a shared repository state, and evaluates recurring details and successive production compactions.

It covers 35 SWE-bench repairs, 146 agentic sessions, and 106 production chains.

Across 14 summarizer backbones, stable damage ranges from 5-64% of 22 full-history successes: Claude Opus 4.8 preserves the most repairs, while DeepSeek-Flash damages the most.

With Kimi-K3 as summarizer, the Kimi Code prompt preserves the most task continuations (19/22), whereas Claude Code retains the most recurring details (15.0%).

LLM summaries retain at most 16.0% of recurring details, compared with 79.1% for mechanical extraction; production summaries retain 15.7% at the first boundary.

Failures recur across methods: one repair is damaged by 13/14 backbones, and another by all 5 prompts.

These results show that reliable compaction requires preserving the progress needed to complete a task, and that retaining relevant information alone does not ensure successful continuation.

Code is available at https://github.com/cai-jianfeng/compact-bench and data is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/caijanfeng/compact-bench.

A9 Selective and Hybrid-State Communication for Latent Multi-Agent Systems

arXiv: 2610.37779 QwenAgentMulti-Agent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Efficient communication remains a major bottleneck in large language model multiagent systems.

Latent communication reduces repeated natural-language exchange by transferring internal model states between agents, but these transfers remain costly and are typically designed around conventional KV-cache representations.

We address this problem from two complementary perspectives.

We introduce SelectKV, a boundary-aware method that selectively communicates KV states while protecting inherited context, and HyLaMAS, which extends latent collaboration to hybrid linear-attention models by communicating both KV caches and recurrent states through a shared workspace.

Across six benchmarks, SelectKV reduces communicated KV positions and logical KV payload by 9.7-9.9%, with accuracy unchanged on two benchmarks, within one percentage point on three, and six points lower on HumanEval+.

Across Qwen3.5 model scales, HyLaMAS reduces decoded output tokens by 53-80% and achieves 1.16-2.78x speedups relative to text-based multi-agent communication.

Together, these independently evaluated methods represent two complementary approaches to efficient latent-state communication across conventional Transformer and hybrid model architectures.

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

While frontier vision-language models demonstrate strong capabilities in user interface (UI) navigation, their high inference costs prohibit everyday use.

Conversely, smaller models trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or offline reinforcement learning often memorize static trajectories, leading to brittle execution and an inability to recover from errors.

To address these limitations, we propose a novel online reinforcement learning framework that trains UI navigation agents directly on live iOS applications.

Our framework decouples the agent into two specialized components: a Planning agent and a UI Grounding model.

Evaluations on rigorous benchmarks, including UINavBench, iOSWorld, and AndroidWorld, demonstrate that our online RL trained agents substantially outperform both SFT and terminal-only RL baselines while accelerating training convergence.

Notably, our 8B RL agents achieve nearly a sevenfold improvement over the SFT baseline in out-of-distribution generalization to Android environments, while outperforming Gemini-3.5-Flash and tying with Gemini-3.1-Pro on real iOS applications.
