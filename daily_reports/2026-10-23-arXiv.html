A2 Same Luck, Different Choice: Twin-World Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv: 2610.31544 QwenAgentic RLAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Agentic reinforcement learning rewards a complete episode, yet the policy needs credit for every decision inside it.

Counterfactual branching estimates this credit by re-running the episode after an alternative decision, but the branch draws its sampling noise independently of the factual episode, so the outcome difference mixes the effect of the decision with the luck of everything that follows.

We observe that in agentic search the retriever, the corpus, and the grader are deterministic, so an episode is a deterministic function of the policy's decisions and its sampling noise.

Twin worlds are therefore realizable: the same noise, a different decision.

We introduce TWICE (TWIn-world Credit Estimation), which keys the Gumbel noise of every token by turn and line, rolls out counterfactual twins that inherit the factual episode's downstream noise, and turns their paired outcome differences into turn-level advantages for policy-gradient updates on actor-sampled trajectories; only the actor is deployed.

Twin credit is unbiased for any coupling quality, its variance is that of an independent counterfactual scaled by $1-\rho$, where $\rho$ is the twins' outcome correlation, and under coupling the number of rollouts needed to resolve an advantage of size $\Delta$ falls from $\Theta(\Delta^{-2})$ to $\Theta(\Delta^{-1})$.

A square-root rule spends the twin budget where outcomes are predicted to disagree per unit cost.

On seven question-answering benchmarks, TWICE improves the average exact match of GRPO by 5.3 and 4.5 points on Qwen2.5-3B and 7B, outperforms tree-search and counterfactual-branching baselines, and uses 68% fewer auxiliary tokens than VinePPO.

A3 Same Beliefs, Different Policies: Learning Reusable Representations for Early Stopping in LLM Reasoning

arXiv: 2610.30450 OpenAIQwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Reasoning LLMs often continue reasoning after reaching a correct answer, wasting computation and sometimes replacing it with an incorrect one.

Deciding when to stop is a metareasoning problem: the model must weigh what further reasoning could achieve against its cost, given what it can already answer.

Direct utility training learns a stopping representation at a chosen token price; supporting new prices can require additional controller training.

We introduce BELT (BELief-based Termination), which separates outcome prediction from stopping control.

It learns beliefs about current and future answers from a frozen LLM's hidden states, then fits a linear stopping policy on the frozen features.

The prediction targets are independent of token price, so one probe supports different accuracy-cost trade-offs through CPU policy refitting or threshold adjustment.

Averaged over five out-of-distribution (OOD) math benchmarks, BELT reduces generated tokens by 41% on Qwen3-1.7B and 51% on OLMo-3-7B-Think without loss of accuracy.

The same math-trained BELT transfers to coding and agentic tasks without training on code or agentic data.

In all three domains, its accuracy-cost trade-offs are competitive with or better than those of end-to-end controllers.

On OLMo3, its probe has 21.2x fewer parameters than one checkpoint of the strongest baseline, OS-Pruner, and needs 55x fewer training GPU-hours than OS-Pruner's five price-specific checkpoints.

A4 Kernel-Managed Shared Memory for System-Wide Personalization

arXiv: 2609.10144 OpenAIMetaQwen

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

AI systems become more useful when they can adapt to the people using them, but in multi-agent systems, useful context learned by one agent often remains unavailable to others.

We present kernel-managed shared memory, a system-level abstraction in which specialized agents write structured, tagged memories while the agent-system kernel, not individual agents, governs retrieval, privacy enforcement, and prompt injection.

We implement and evaluate this design on AIOS and compare it against three alternatives across three assistant models (GPT-4o, Llama-3.1:8B, Qwen-2.5:7B) and 1,800 total trials.

Against an unmanaged external memory backend (Mem0) using identical underlying storage, kernel-managed retrieval and injection improve personalization scores by 2.4-4.0 points on a 5-point scale (e.g., 1.05 to 4.69 profile usage on GPT-4o), with every comparison significant at p < 10^-18.

Against standard retrieval-augmented injection, gains are similarly large and consistent across all three models.

Against full, unfiltered context concatenation, a soft ceiling on available context rather than on response quality, kernel-managed injection statistically matches performance on two of three models and shows a small, model-specific deficit on the third, while using substantially shorter prompts: end-to-end latency is 15-61% lower across all three models, with corresponding reductions in per-call token usage and inference cost.

These results indicate that centralizing memory management in the agent-system kernel, rather than leaving retrieval and privacy enforcement to individual agents, delivers most of the personalization benefit of unconstrained context at a fraction of its cost.

A6 MPIDetector: Detecting Multi-modal Prompt Injection via LLM-based Reasoning

arXiv: 2610.30202 GoogleQwenByteDance

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

With the rapid integration of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into real-world applications, Prompt Injection (PI) has emerged as a critical security threat.

Attackers can conceal malicious instructions within textual narratives or visual inputs to bypass safety alignment.

Existing approaches, such as signature- and perplexity-based methods, rely heavily on pattern matching, making them insufficient to detect sophisticated, cross-modal PI payloads.

In this paper, we propose a novel detection framework, called MPIDetector, that leverages LLMs' intrinsic reasoning capabilities via few-shot In-Context Learning (ICL).

Instead of fine-tuning, we provide the model with specific prompt templates and representative attack examples, enabling it to scrutinize the semantic intent of input across modalities.

We constructed a comprehensive evaluation benchmark comprising eight textual and seven visual malicious categories, supplemented with benign datasets to rigorously test for false positives.

Our evaluation across multiple leading LLMs, including Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, and Doubao-seed-1.6-flash, demonstrates the efficacy and generalizability of this approach.

The proposed method achieves up to 90.91% accuracy and 100% recall on textual tasks.

In the visual domain, it achieves up to 99.28% accuracy while maintaining 100% precision across all evaluated models.

Crucially, when evaluated against 500 highly obfuscated environmental web-agent injections (WebInject), MPIDetector achieved a detection rate up to 99.80%.

Comparisons with two baseline approaches for detecting textual PI payloads, along with two additional methods for visual input detection, demonstrate MPIDetector's superior accuracy.

These results show that LLM-driven reasoning provides a robust, cross-modal defense against advanced prompt injections.

In this work, we present a unified training framework for automatic PPT generation toward presentation intelligence, integrating cross-validated data synthesis, guided teacher enhancement, and verifiable reward optimization.

A8 SWE-VERIFIER: Selective Verification for Cost-Efficient Coding Agents

arXiv: 2610.30284 DeepSeekQwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Coding agents spend substantial computation generating actions and processing long interaction histories.

Poor action choices can steer agents into low-value states, wasting computation and increasing API costs.

Sampling and verifying alternative actions can improve the agent's decisions, but applying this procedure at every step adds substantial overhead.

We introduce SWE-Verifier, a lightweight generative model that selectively triggers candidate sampling and verification.

It scores the coding agent's proposed action to determine when additional candidate sampling is needed, then ranks the resulting actions before execution.

We train SWE-Verifier from Qwen3.5-4B using preference data that groups contrasting actions with their resulting environment observations and rubric-guided teacher scores.

Using the mini-swe-agent harness, SWE-Verifier improves resolution from 75.47% to 77.20% on MiniMax-M2.7 at 1.09 times cost and generalizes to DeepSeek-V4-Flash without training on its actions, improving resolution from 72.60% to 74.27% at 1.13 times cost.

Across both models, SWE-Verifier verifies fewer than 4% of steps and achieves comparable or higher resolution than the trajectory and all-step baselines at substantially lower cost.

These results demonstrate that selective candidate sampling and lightweight verification provide complementary gains in coding-agent performance and inference efficiency.

Our model, code, and data are available at https://github.com/zkjzou/SWE-Verifier.

A9 nnInteractive Agent: General-Purpose AI Agents as 3D Image Annotators

arXiv: 2610.31080 AnthropicAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Interactive 3D segmentation models such as nnInteractive turn a few spatial prompts (points, scribbles, boxes) into accurate volumetric masks, but a human still does the annotation work: finding each structure, placing prompts, checking the result and correcting it.

General-purpose AI agents can now interpret images, call tools and write code.

We show that such agents can take over this work without additional training: the user makes a request in plain language, and the agent works out what has to be segmented, locates the targets, prompts nnInteractive, inspects the 3D result, corrects it and responds to feedback.

Asked for 'the muscles used for chewing', an agent identifies and segments the masseters and pterygoids; asked to count liver lesions, it writes its own code to find candidates and splits a merged lesion on request.

Our open-source nninteractive-agent provides nnInteractive as tools over the Model Context Protocol (MCP), together with a skill that encodes how experienced users prompt it.

On a benchmark spanning CT, MRI, PET and micro-CT of humans, a mouse and an ant (14 images; 51 targets from organs and tumours to muscles, foot bones, vessel trees and an insect brain), Claude Code and Codex reach a mean Dice of 0.81 and 0.84 from the request alone; when told where each target is, both reach 0.85 and prompt nearly as effectively as an oracle that places prompts using the reference segmentation.

A text-prompted 3D model (VoxTell) is more accurate on most standard radiological targets but less robust: it returns an empty mask for one of them and fails on the leg muscles, the foot bones, the mouse and the ant, where the agents remain accurate.

A12 TRCA: Transition-wise Rubric Credit Assignment for Long-horizon LLM Agents

arXiv: 2608.16156 QwenAgentLLM

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Long-horizon large language model (LLM) agents are typically optimized with sparse terminal outcomes, making fine-grained credit assignment across multi-step interactions difficult.
