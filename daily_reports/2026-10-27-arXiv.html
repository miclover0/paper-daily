📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

In this study, we explore the potential of state of the art multi-modal (reasoning) large language models (LLMs) for deepfake image detection such as (OpenAI O1/4o, Gemini thinking Flash 2, Deepseek Janus, Grok 3, llama 3.2, Qwen 2/2.5 VL, Mistral Pixtral, Claude 3.5/3.7 sonnet) . 在这项研究中，我们探讨了最先进的多模态（推理）大型语言模型（ LLM ）用于深度伪造图像检测的潜力，例如（ OpenAI O1/4o ， Gemini thinking Flash 2 ， Deepseek Janus ， Grok 3 ， llama 3.2 ， Qwen 2/2 .5 VL ， Mistral Pixtral ， Claude 3.5/3 .7 sonnet ）。

To enhance performance, we employ prompt tuning and conduct an in-depth analysis of the models' reasoning pathways to identify key contributing factors in their decision-making process.

Our findings indicate that best multi-modal LLMs achieve competitive performance with promising generalization ability with zero shot, even surpass traditional deepfake detection pipelines in out-of-distribution datasets while the rest of the LLM families performs extremely disappointing with some worse than random guess. 我们的研究结果表明，最好的多模态LLM在零拍摄的情况下通过有希望的泛化能力实现了竞争性能，甚至在分布外的数据集中超过了传统的deepfake检测管道，而其余的LLM系列在比随机猜测更差的情况下表现非常令人失望。

This study highlights the potential of integrating multi-modal reasoning in future deepfake detection frameworks and provides insights into model interpretability for robustness in real-world scenarios. 本研究强调了在未来的deepfake检测框架中集成多模态推理的潜力，并提供了对现实场景中鲁棒性的模型可解释性的见解。

A2 Can Multi-modal (reasoning) LLMs detect document manipulation?

arXiv: 2508.11021 OpenAIAnthropicGoogle

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Document fraud poses a significant threat to industries reliant on secure and verifiable documentation, necessitating robust detection mechanisms.

This study investigates the efficacy of state-of-the-art multi-modal large language models (LLMs)-including OpenAI O1, OpenAI 4o, Gemini Flash (thinking), Deepseek Janus, Grok, Llama 3.2 and 4, Qwen 2 and 2.5 VL, Mistral Pixtral, and Claude 3.5 and 3.7 Sonnet-in detecting fraudulent documents. 本研究调查了最先进的多模态大型语言模型（ LLM ） （包括OpenAI O1、OpenAI 4o、Gemini Flash （思维）、Deepseek Janus、Grok、Llama 3.2和4、Qwen 2和2.5 VL、Mistral Pixtral以及Claude 3.5和3.7 Sonnet ）在检测欺诈性文档方面的功效。

We benchmark these models against each other and prior work on document fraud detection techniques using a standard dataset with real transactional documents. 我们使用具有真实交易文档的标准数据集，将这些模型相互对比，并先前在文档欺诈检测技术方面的工作。

Through prompt optimization and detailed analysis of the models' reasoning processes, we evaluate their ability to identify subtle indicators of fraud, such as tampered text, misaligned formatting, and inconsistent transactional sums.

Our results reveal that top-performing multi-modal LLMs demonstrate superior zero-shot generalization, outperforming conventional methods on out-of-distribution datasets, while several vision LLMs exhibit inconsistent or subpar performance.

Notably, model size and advanced reasoning capabilities show limited correlation with detection accuracy, suggesting task-specific fine-tuning is critical. 值得注意的是，模型大小和高级推理能力与检测准确性的相关性有限，这表明特定任务的微调至关重要。

This study underscores the potential of multi-modal LLMs in enhancing document fraud detection systems and provides a foundation for future research into interpretable and scalable fraud mitigation strategies. 本研究强调了多模式LLM在增强文档欺诈检测系统方面的潜力，并为未来研究可解释和可扩展的欺诈缓解策略奠定了基础。

A3 Asking the World: Generalist Physical Reasoning through Agentic World Modeling and Probing

arXiv: 2609.39135 OpenAIGoogleAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Physical reasoning from video requires inferring latent physical properties and dynamics beyond direct observation.

Direct VLM inference remains unreliable on complex physical tasks without explicit modeling and validation, while predefined tool pipelines rely on task- and domain-specific priors that limit generalization across materials, dynamics, and reasoning tasks. 在没有明确建模和验证的情况下，直接VLM推理在复杂的物理任务上仍然不可靠，而预定义的工具管道依赖于特定于任务和领域的先验，这些先验限制了跨材料、动态和推理任务的泛化。

We introduce Asking the World (ATW), a generalist agent that constructs and interrogates task-relevant executable worlds through two adaptive stages: World Modeling calibrates a world from video, while World Probing queries, simulates, and intervenes on it to obtain question-relevant evidence.

Rather than prescribing the operations in either stage, ATW determines how to model and probe according to the scene and question. ATW不是规定任何一个阶段的操作，而是根据场景和问题确定如何建模和探测。

We develop PolyWorld Engine, a lightweight and highly programmable Warp-based multiphysics simulator for constructing and probing worlds with rigid bodies, soft bodies, cloth, ropes, fluids, and their coupled interactions. 我们开发了PolyWorld Engine ，这是一款轻量级、高度可编程的基于扭曲的多物理场模拟器，用于构建和探测具有刚体、软体、布、绳索、流体及其耦合相互作用的世界。

CEM-based system identification recovers task-relevant dynamics during World Modeling. 基于CEM的系统识别在世界建模过程中恢复与任务相关的动态。

The resulting world becomes an active workspace for question-directed physical experiments rather than a predetermined downstream tool.

We evaluate ATW on CLEVRER, ContPhy, and three real-world scenarios.

Using Gemini-3-Flash as its base VLM, ATW achieves 80.82% overall per-question accuracy on CLEVRER, improving direct Gemini-3-Flash by 46.50 points, GPT-5.5 by 13.58 points, and PhysMind by 8.27 points. 使用Gemini-3-Flash作为其基础VLM ， ATW在CLEVRER上实现了80.82 ％的整体每次提问准确率，将直接Gemini-3-Flash提高了46.50分， GPT-5.5提高了13.58分， PhysMind提高了8.27分。

On ContPhy, it reaches 70.56% overall accuracy, surpassing Gemini-3-Flash by 28.10 points and GPT-5.5 by 3.53 points.

Across the three real-world scenarios, ATW achieves 71.67% accuracy, 28.33 points above GPT-5.5.

These results establish agentic world modeling and probing as an effective, execution-grounded approach to generalist physical reasoning.

A4 RSI-Opt: Recursive Self-Improvement Optimizer Agents For Model Training

arXiv: 2610.32026 OpenAIRSIAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Conventional neural-network training typically follows a predefined optimization recipe, motivating a LLM agent that monitors training dynamics and adjusts optimizer settings as training progresses, but a fixed agent policy does not accumulate experience across runs.

We introduce RSI-Opt, a plug-and-play Recursive Self-Improvement Optimizer Agents which evolves the knowledge that guides this process: a prompt, a playbook of procedures, a library of tested optimizer code, and a decision cadence.

A meta-agent rewrites this policy using evidence from earlier runs, and an archive retains evaluated policies for further evolution.

Across 5 generations on three training tasks, the meta-agents develop reusable procedures and code and conduct their own CPU proxy experiments.

RSI-Opt substantially improves optimizer-agent performance, with representative raw-space summary $z$-scores increasing from $+10.48$ to $+14.33$ on the evolution suite with Opus 5.5 and from $-0.36$ to $+3.11$ on held-out tasks with GPT-5.5 through cross-model policy transfer.

On the Burgers PDE, it reduces mean relative $L_2$ error by approximately 2.7-fold with Opus 5.5 and 8.8-fold with GPT-6.1 over the fixed-prompt agent baseline, although PDE transfer varies across LLMs and tasks.

Controlled comparisons show that the evolved policy combines reusable optimization knowledge with adaptation to each task: initial decisions capture most observed gains on seen tasks, while continued adaptation improves outcomes on unseen tasks.

These findings demonstrate how procedures and executable tools accumulated across training runs can improve optimizer-agent performance and support transfer to scientific machine learning.

A5 TGR-EvoDAG: A DAG-based Multi-Agent Code Evolution Framework for Generative Recommendation on Tencent

arXiv: 2610.32326 HunyuanRSIAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Large Language Model (LLM) agents have enabled automated code evolution for algorithm discovery.

However, current recursive self-improvement (RSI) research for sequential recommendation faces a dilemma: general-purpose frameworks lack domain-specific feedback, while recommender-specific frameworks rely on linear evolution.

Crucially, naively integrating them fails when applied to Generative Recommendation (GR).

Unlike end-to-end models, GR relies on a module-cascaded pipeline (tokenization, pre-training, and alignment).

In this paradigm, existing evolution methods suffer from: i) cost blindness, squandering budgets by ignoring stage-asymmetric retraining overheads; and ii) short-sighted evaluation, discarding promising upstream innovations that temporarily regress metrics before downstream adaptation.

To bridge these gaps, we propose TGR-EvoDAG, a multi-agent code evolution framework tailored for GR.

TGR-EvoDAG models code evolution as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) to maintain multiple evolution branches and merge complementary innovations, unifying advanced search with rich GR diagnostic feedback.

We further introduce stage-aware cost planning to optimize GPU-hour budgets, and active downstream co-adaptation to navigate temporary regressions and unlock cross-stage gains.

On public Amazon datasets, TGR-EvoDAG enables the first autonomous evolution of a complete GR pipeline, improving offline metrics by up to 37.4% and Agent4Rec simulated user satisfaction by 19.7% over the strongest baseline.

Furthermore, in industrial deployment on Tencent's large-scale commercial media platform, the evolved GR achieves a 12.16% relative improvement in offline Hit@1 and a statistically significant 0.40% increase in online click-through rate (CTR).

A6 HarnessMM: Recursive Self-Improvement for Harness Token Efficiency

arXiv: 2610.32813 DeepSeekRSIAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Agent harnesses repeatedly invoke reasoning after tool feedback, even when an earlier call has already fixed the next action.

We introduce HarnessMM (Harness Minus Minus), a method for improving harness token efficiency through guarded decision reuse and recursive self-improvement (RSI).

RSI iteratively refines companion harness configurations from training feedback, with validation selecting the configuration for deployment.

A controller at the model-tool boundary reuses resolved decisions to replace later reasoning callbacks while actor weights remain fixed.

Executable Decision Graphs store exact actions, literal arguments, causal dependencies, and public guards.

Guarded Decision Reuse activates these persistent decisions from observed receipts; Certified Callback Substitution executes them through the native interface and records removal of the registered slow callback.

New semantic decisions, unsupported evidence, and selector abstention invoke the original actor again.

The representation restricts what feedback may change, while complete-task measurements establish quality and cost.

On HumanEval, HarnessMM preserves Codex success at 90.82\% while reducing Tokens/task (k) from 66.72 to 47.02 (29.54\% saving).

For DeepSeek Harness, success rises from 71.43\% to 85.71\% while Tokens/task (k) fall from 96.28 to 11.62 (87.93\% saving).

A separate MBPP ablation measures 26.91\% lower cost than single-use graphs and 19.52\% lower cost than retained callbacks.

These results establish guarded decision reuse as a practical way to reduce repeated reasoning in existing harnesses.

A7 An Agentic Approach with Evidence-Grounded Verification for Multimodal Emotion Reasoning in Conversation

arXiv: 2610.31685 MetaQwenAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

LLM-based multimodal emotion reasoning systems perform well on standard benchmarks, but improving them through instruction tuning can be computationally expensive.

Agentic pipelines built from frozen models avoid this cost, but introduce a different challenge: when a prediction is wrong, the system must identify which agent or stage caused the error.

We address this problem with an agentic framework for traceable multimodal emotion reasoning and prompt-space adaptation.

Modality-specific perception agents analyze visual, acoustic, and lexical inputs and post evidence-cited observations to a shared board.

An integration agent combines these observations with retrieved multimodal examples to produce an emotion description, label set, and supporting citations, while an MLLM critic can request one revision before the output is finalized.

For incorrect predictions, a separate evidence-grounded verification stage applies deterministic checks to verify that cited evidence and references are valid, and an entailment check to determine whether the final claims are supported by that evidence.

Detected failures are then grouped by the responsible component and failure type, converted into targeted prompt patches, and accepted only after validation on a held-out set.

This adapts the agent prompts without updating model weights.

On the MER2025 dataset, targeted prompt adaptation outperforms the frozen static pipeline on all four MLLMs, ranging from 2B to 38B parameters, and matches or outperforms undifferentiated reflection baselines on each of them: VideoLLaMA3-2B, Phi-3.5-vision-instruct, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, and InternVL2.5-38B.

It improves F-score by 0.6--2.2 points depending on the backbone, including an increase from 52.3 to 54.5 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

A8 Compact-Bench: Measuring What Context Compaction Costs Long-Horizon Coding Agents

arXiv: 2610.31710 AnthropicDeepSeekKimi

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Context compaction lets long-horizon coding agents continue beyond their context windows, but can erase the state needed to finish a task.

Prior evaluations measure efficiency, retained information, or end-to-end success, leaving the effect of a specific history replacement difficult to isolate.

Compact-Bench pairs full-history and summary-only continuations from a shared repository state, and evaluates recurring details and successive production compactions.

It covers 35 SWE-bench repairs, 146 agentic sessions, and 106 production chains.

Across 14 summarizer backbones, stable damage ranges from 5-64% of 22 full-history successes: Claude Opus 4.8 preserves the most repairs, while DeepSeek-Flash damages the most.

With Kimi-K3 as summarizer, the Kimi Code prompt preserves the most task continuations (19/22), whereas Claude Code retains the most recurring details (15.0%).

LLM summaries retain at most 16.0% of recurring details, compared with 79.1% for mechanical extraction; production summaries retain 15.7% at the first boundary.

Failures recur across methods: one repair is damaged by 13/14 backbones, and another by all 5 prompts.

These results show that reliable compaction requires preserving the progress needed to complete a task, and that retaining relevant information alone does not ensure successful continuation.

Code is available at https://github.com/cai-jianfeng/compact-bench and data is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/caijanfeng/compact-bench.

A9 Tool-Calling LLM Agents Under Realistic MCP Faults: Scale Does Not Purchase Fault Tolerance

arXiv: 2610.32555 OpenAIAgentLLM

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

LLM agents increasingly rely on external tools exposed via the Model Context Protocol (MCP), yet current benchmarks assume tools respond correctly and on time, leaving agent resilience to realistic tool failures untested.

We introduce MCP-Injector, a drop-in, protocol-aware fault-injection middleware that interposes on the MCP tool-call path with controlled persistence (permanent, transient, intermittent) and seedable schedules.

Its fault model is calibrated to the observed incident distribution (failure mode x persistence) of the public MCP ecosystem, mined from 57,240 issue and pull-request threads across 1,774 open-source MCP repositories.

In a 1,350-cell experiment crossing two backbone sizes (20B, 120B), three agent stacks (custom multi-round plan-and-execute, OpenAI Agents SDK, LangChain), and three fault injection conditions, we find that faults drawn from this permanent-heavy stress profile cause severe quality degradation (task-completion score falls by 60%, from 8.08 to 3.23 out of 10), and that scale does not purchase fault tolerance for the two gpt-oss backbones we test: the 120B retains no larger share of its fault-free score under faults (resilience-ratio model, $p = 0.96$), and its completion rate under faults is, if anything, lower (91.1% vs.

95.6%; difference 4.4 points, 95% CI $[-0.2, 9.3]$).

Agent stacks differ in how well they absorb faults, which suggests that framework-level error handling matters more than model scale.
