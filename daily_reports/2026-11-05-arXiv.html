A1 EmoReward: Self-Evolving Reward Calibration for Emotional Video Generation RL

arXiv: 2611.06518 HunyuanAgentic RLRSI

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has advanced generative model post-training in verifiable domains such as code and mathematics, but evaluating video quality, especially emotional expression, often depends on subjective aesthetics and has no uniquely correct target. 组相对策略优化（ GRPO ）在代码和数学等可验证领域具有先进的生成模型后训练，但评估视频质量，特别是情感表达，通常取决于主观美学，并且没有唯一正确的目标。

Existing approaches typically rely on fixed rewards for visual quality, motion quality, and text alignment that poorly capture emotional intent and can invite reward hacking, while RLHF is difficult to scale and subject to individual aesthetic differences that complicate the calibration of facial, body motion, and chromatic cues across narrative contexts.

We introduce EmoReward, a new self-evolving training mechanism that distills evidence reliability from reward traces collected during an actual training run to adaptively guide multi-reward weight allocation in subsequent training by determining which emotional evidence to trust and how much to weight it.

EmoReward first plans specific grounding cues from the emotional prompt to guide evaluation, then uses heterogeneous signal primitives to score the generated video during the act stage.

Reflective Reward Gating calibrates their contributions through instance-level gates for phase, face confidence, conflict, and subtlety, then renormalizes weights over the active set to produce the sample's fused reward.

RewardMemory then estimates each primitive's reliability from rollout scores and outcomes within each emotional regime in the finished training run, then fixes bounded multipliers that calibrate fusion weights in the next training run without updating reward models.

Experiments on HunyuanVideo 1.5 show improvements across multiple RL algorithms and compare EmoReward with other agentic reward methods under SAGE-GRPO.

Ablations and transfer experiments on SD 3.5 and FLUX further support adaptive reward calibration for visual generation.

A2 ParaVT: Taming the Tool Prior Paradox for Parallel Tool Use in Agentic Video Reinforcement Learning

arXiv: 2605.20342 QwenAgentMulti-Agent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Training large multimodal models (LMMs) via reinforcement learning (RL) to natively invoke video-processing tools (e.g., cropping) has become a promising route to long-video understanding.

However, most existing agentic Video-LMMs dispatch tool calls sequentially (i.e., one per turn).

Sequential tool calling can propagate localization errors, accumulate irrelevant context, and require an additional main-model generation for each successive crop.

We introduce ParaVT, a multi-agent RL framework for Parallel Video Tool calling, dispatching multiple time-window crops in a single turn, allowing evidence from multiple temporal windows to be examined concurrently.

Yet applying standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to ParaVT reveals an obstacle we term the Tool Prior Paradox: inherited tool-use priors appear to support exploration while competing with the output format learned during supervised fine-tuning.

Standard RL can also settle on tool skipping.

A comparison with a model with weaker tool-use priors shows stable formatting but no tool calls during RL, consistent with a trade-off between format stability and tool exploration.

We propose PARA-GRPO (Parseability-Anchored and Ratio-gAted GRPO), which augments standard RL with two complementary mechanisms: (i) a format reward that encourages tag closure and penalizes unclosed reasoning blocks, and (ii) a per-prompt randomization of the overview-frame budget to create a reward advantage for tool use when the overview provides insufficient evidence.

Across six long-video understanding benchmarks, ParaVT improves over the Qwen3-VL baseline by +7.9% on average, with PARA-GRPO raising training-time format reward from 0.13 to a peak of 0.64.

These findings highlight the need to account for inherited tool-use priors when designing RL methods for long-video understanding.

A3 Payoff scaling shapes cooperation in LLM agents across languages

arXiv: 2601.19082 AnthropicGoogleAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous agents that negotiate, coordinate, and make decisions on behalf of human users.

Understanding how they cooperate is therefore important for AI governance and machine behaviour research.

We examine two factors: payoff magnitude and interaction language.

Classical game theory predicts that multiplying all payoffs by a positive constant should not change behaviour because the underlying strategic incentives remain unchanged.

We test this prediction using five widely adopted commercial LLMs playing an iterated prisoner's dilemma across ten payoff scales and five languages (English, French, Vietnamese, Chinese and Arabic).

Contrary to theoretical expectations, changing only the numerical scale of payoffs substantially alters cooperation.

Models respond differently, often in opposite directions, meaning that averages across models can conceal important behavioural heterogeneity.

Sensitivity to payoff scale also depends strongly on prompt language.

Moreover, payoff scale modifies the effects of persona instructions: at sub-unit stakes, where no payoff shown exceeds 1, all five models cooperate more under a selfish than a cooperative persona, whereas Claude and Gemini reverse this pattern at larger scales.

These findings show that cooperation measures depend critically on payoff scale and language and should not be treated as fixed model traits.

A4 StoreBench: A Live-Commerce Environment for Evaluating and Training Autonomous Operator Agents

arXiv: 2610.10942 AnthropicDeepSeekQwen

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Reinforcement learning environments are now a primary lever for improving large language model (LLM) capabilities in post-training, yet most agentic benchmarks remain static: the world moves only when the agent acts, the reward is a terminal verdict, and the pass bar is set arbitrarily.

We introduce StoreBench, a live-commerce environment in which an agent runs a mid-size online apparel store on a production-grade commerce backend, testing long-horizon planning and economic judgment under uncertainty.

Customers order around the clock, suppliers reprice and fail, and market shocks arrive with partial or no warning.

The agent acts through the same 29 merchant tools a human operator would use, under a windowed operation budget that makes simulated time a function of actions taken, so model latency cannot influence simulated time.

Pass thresholds are calibrated against scripted anchor policies, the reward is hardened against a catalogue of reward hacks, and every episode replays identically given a sequence of actions.

We evaluate seven frontier LLMs on 11 scenarios of 30 to 45 days and a full simulated year, over three world seeds at matched reasoning effort.

No model matches the scripted smart-triage policy on average: the best, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, passes 49% of task-seed cells against the heuristic's 97%.

Human experts working through the same tools and budgets outscore every model (mean composite 0.708 vs.

0.700).

Over a full simulated year under the Claude Code harness, most models show dramatic performance improvement.

In a GRPO post-training run, Qwen3.5-27B trained on only five disjoint tasks raises its mean composite on the held-out evaluation tasks from 0.136 to 0.373.

We release five example training-split tasks, ten sample trajectories, and the scoring and verification tooling; the full environment and evaluation suite are withheld to keep the benchmark uncontaminated.

A5 Iterated creation, grading, and revision of data science projects by language models

arXiv: 2611.05548 OpenAIAnthropicGoogle

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

We evaluate the extent to which agentic AI models can complete an end-to-end data science project, and how this improves with feedback.

A 'writer' model with specific tools creates a complete data science project including downloading, cleaning, and analyzing a dataset, and writing a short paper about it.

The project is executed and a 'grader' model evaluates it.

The writer can then revise its submission, and this repeats for a total of three rounds.

We consider seven models from five developers: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek, and Z.AI.

Each model is both a writer and a grader, so there are 49 writer-grader pairs and we run each pair three times.

Of the 147 initial submissions, 107 run and render, and the average score increases from 59 per cent to 76 per cent after the final round of feedback.

By writer the extent of the increase ranges from 3 percentage points for Anthropic's Opus 5.5 to 46 percentage points for Anthropic's Haiku 4.5, and by grader it ranges from 13 percentage points for Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash to 32 percentage points for Anthropic's Haiku 4.5.

When a model grades its own writing the score tends to be about 9 percentage points higher than the average of what the other models give it.

About half of the difference that we see from round to round is within the grader's own noise.

Feedback generally results in the highest marks, but even just having the chance to revise, or having the additional budget, in terms of time or tool calls, without any feedback, results in considerable increases for some of the models but not for others.

We find that how much such a loop helps depends on which model plays which role, but weaker models generally do not improve stronger models.

A6 PAYWORTHY: Can LLMs Predict Which Freelance Jobs an AI Agent Will Complete?

arXiv: 2611.05759 AnthropicQwenKimi

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Before an AI agent takes on a paid job, someone has to judge whether it is likely to succeed.

We test whether frontier language models can make this call before any attempt.

We propose PAYWORTHY which contains 1{,}494 real freelance job postings, sampled from 18.8M postings made between 2020 and 2026 on a freelancing platform.

An agent attempts each job from the posting alone in a single run, and an LLM judge acting as the client decides whether to accept the work.

We ask 18 models to forecast how likely the agent is to succeed and measure how well each model's forecasts rank the accepted jobs above the rejected ones (AUC, where 0.5 is a random ranking).

The answer depends on how the agent is described.

Told what the agent cannot do, every model ranks the jobs well (AUC 0.66--0.86).

Told instead that it is a generic agent with a browser and code execution, the same models rank the same jobs about as well as a random ordering (0.45--0.56).

These results hold under judges from four other model families (Claude, GLM, Kimi and Qwen), and the informed forecasts also rank the verdicts of two human raters.

Code is available at https://github.com/upwork/Payworthy

A7 Reglos: A Self-Evolving Personal Memory Agent with Recursive Self-Improvement

arXiv: 2611.06049 OpenAIQwenRSI

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Personal agents draw on past conversations to tailor their responses to each user.

Their memory systems store and update what each user says, and coding agents now also rewrite the harness that turns this memory into the model's context.

Such patches risk overfitting, because one tested only on the failures it targets can fix those cases and help nowhere else.

We present Reglos, a training-free personal memory agent that tests whether harness changes help beyond the failures they were written to fix.

Its self-evolving memory adds to each user's append-only record with every conversation, as new preferences supersede old ones and facts can be forgotten.

Its harness improves through recursive self-improvement, in which a coding agent proposes patches from failure traces and a critic from a second model family screens each one for benchmark gaming.

After the harness is frozen, a fixed evaluator decides which further patches to accept.

With the same backbone behind every system, Reglos outperforms state-of-the-art memory systems such as Mem0, MemOS and EverOS, reaching 61.3% and 84.0% on PersonaMem-v2 and LongMemEval-S with Qwen3.8-27B, and 57.5% and 87.2% with GPT-5.6 Luna.

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Active embodied question answering requires agents to acquire evidence through viewpoint changes and physical interaction.

The central challenge is to avoid undirected interaction: taking plausible actions without specifying what evidence they should reveal for answering the question.

Action plans and accumulated histories alone can leave the informational purpose of an interaction implicit.

We introduce EviSeek, a training-free framework that makes question-derived evidence demands explicit acquisition objectives, linking observation-conditioned actions and selectively retained evidence to the information the agent seeks.

These findings support making the informational purpose of interaction explicit, with action selection and evidence retention organized around what the agent needs to learn to answer the question.

A9 CompressionBench: How can your coding agents compress LLMs?

arXiv: 2611.06858 RSIAgentLLM

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Recursive self-improvement requires AI systems to improve the models and infrastructure used by subsequent systems.

LLM compression offers a concrete efficiency objective: retain a pretrained model's behavior within a smaller model storage budget.

We study whether coding agents can carry out this step autonomously.

We formulate compression as a terminal task in which an agent receives a source checkpoint, storage limit, inference environment, and time budget, then constructs compressed weights and executable inference code.

The agent controls its algorithm and experiments; perplexity feedback guides search, and independent verification checks the final model.

Across six agents and six model--size settings, 29 of 36 submissions pass verification.

Selecting the lowest-perplexity agent design per task yields an average 3.4% perplexity increase over BF16 and 77.33% downstream accuracy, compared with 79.56% for BF16.

Recorded experiments reveal how agents achieve these outcomes and where improvement breaks down.

Agents lower PPL in 21 of 24 revisions at nearly fixed storage; complete-model feedback reverses 17 of 79 prior local rankings; and generation and inference tests expose failures that likelihood alone misses.

The results identify representation design, experimental judgment, and capability validation as central to agent-driven model compression, a measurable component of efficiency-oriented self-improvement.

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

The models' interpretative potential was evaluated using a mixed-methods approach.

In terms of content validity assessed against raw data by high-level experts in the blinded mode, the performance of Gemini 2.5 Pro was indistinguishable from that of humans.

The comparison of semantic vector embeddings showed that its style was also the closest to humans.

A11 AlgoPilot: Cross-Paradigm Reasoning in Language Models via Strategy Selection and Guidance

arXiv: 2611.04079 GoogleQwenLLM

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Real-world problems span diverse domains, including program synthesis, symbolic reasoning, planning, and optimization, and often require fundamentally different solution paradigms.

A central challenge for LLM-based reasoning is twofold: identifying an appropriate problem-solving approach and formulating it correctly.

In this paper, we propose AlgoPilot, a cross-paradigm reasoning framework that enables adaptive selection of the solving approaches and structured problem formulation prior to execution.

The AlgoPilot framework contains three components: 1) a SteerLM, trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), that adaptively selects the solving strategy (paradigm and modes) based on input problems, 2) the corresponding expert GuideLM, trained via SFT, that generates structured formulation guidance tailored to the selected strategy, and 3) an ExecutionLM that generates solutions conditioned on both the selected strategy and its formulation.

Unlike prompting-based methods that rely on fixed reasoning templates, our approach learns to adaptively choose and structure problem-solving strategies based on the input.

We build AlgoPilot on Qwen3-8B and evaluate across 5 problem categories and 13 benchmarks with 66 unique domains, comparing against both the latest similarly scaled small models and large LLMs.

AlgoPilot significantly improves average accuracy for the same model from 38.4% to 72.9%, outperforming the best large LLM prompting baseline GLM-5 by 11.7%.

Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each component.

Notably, we show that the learned steering and formulation modules can transfer across execution models, suggesting their potential to improve other LLMs.
