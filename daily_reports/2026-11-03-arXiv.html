A1 Reimagining computational quantum research with AI scientists

arXiv: 2611.02833 AnthropicDeepSeekRSI

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

AI scientists are expanding automated research across scientific domains, yet their ability to turn the scientific record into a foundation for autonomous discovery remains elusive.

Here we uncover a fundamental barrier to this transition and demonstrate a route to overcome it in computational quantum science, where advances shape both what we can learn from complex quantum systems and what quantum computing can ultimately deliver.

To identify this barrier, we introduce OmniQ, an expert-audited virtual research environment that converts published studies into executable reproduction tasks.

Across 59 papers spanning classical simulation, quantum algorithms and quantum-property characterization, we systematically evaluate five agentic systems powered by large language models and six third-party agent harnesses.

Our findings reveal that current AI scientists often fail to reproduce published results, exposing a fundamental mismatch between what scientific papers communicate and what autonomous agents require.

By contrast, human expert intervention can turn these failures into successful reproductions, showing that the missing information can be identified and converted into reusable research experience.

Guided by this observation, we develop Residual Agents (RA), a quantum-tailored self-improving AI scientist that distils externally verified workflow repairs into such experience.

RA substantially improves research performance under fixed base models and limited adaptation budgets.

For example, RA raises DeepSeek V4.1 Flash performance from 68% to 85%, matching Claude Code with Opus 5 at a third of its cost despite using a weaker base model.

We integrate all reproduced results into an extensible platform built on the model context protocol, making verified research workflows directly accessible to AI scientists and allowing new scientific papers to be continuously incorporated.

A2 Fooling Reward Models with Gibberish: Adversarial Examples Beyond the Semantic Regime

arXiv: 2604.02686 OpenAIMetaQwen

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Reward models (RMs) are widely used as optimization targets in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

We show that leading RMs can assign higher scores to gibberish than to meaningful reference answers.

To systematically discover such inputs, we introduce Token Mapping Perturbation Attack (TOMPA), a framework that uses a perturbed token mapping to extend an attack LLM's search beyond natural language.

The mapping changes the linguistic meaning of the attack LLM's natural language responses, producing non-linguistic text for the target RM to score.

We then use these scalar scores as rewards to train the attack LLM through reinforcement learning to discover non-linguistic adversarial inputs.

Experiments against Llama and Qwen RMs demonstrate such attacks using two instantiations: identity token mapping across different tokenizers and random permutation with a shared tokenizer.

Against Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B, ranked first on RewardBench 2, the two mappings achieve mean rewards of +33.64 and +43.78, respectively, compared with +17.48 for GPT-5 reference answers.

Their attack success rates are 97.6% and 100%, measured as the percentages of individual responses scoring above their corresponding GPT-5 references.

These results show that strong benchmark performance does not ensure robustness to non-linguistic adversarial inputs, exposing a fundamental vulnerability in using RMs as optimization targets.

A4 SGR-OPSD: Learning from Subgoal Plans with Graph-Regularized Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

arXiv: 2611.02414 QwenAgentic RLAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

OPSD provides dense supervision for agentic reinforcement learning through privileged information feedback.

However, existing privileged information often lacks explicit task-progression planning, while teacher-student log-probability gaps reflect shifts in model preference rather than true task progress.

To address these limitations, we propose Subgoal-Guided Graph-Regularized OPSD (SGR-OPSD), a framework that combines subgoal supervision with interaction-derived evidence for turn-level credit assignment.

Specifically, SGR-OPSD uses LLM-generated subgoal plans to provide dense action-level guidance, converting them into advantage weights via a constrained, KL-regularized objective.

To ground credit in observed outcomes, we construct a Subgoal Progress Graph that estimates empirical returns from sampled transition frequencies.

The graph compares empirical action returns against the state average to derive preferences that calibrate the teacher's credit weights.

Evaluated on ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA across two model scales (Qwen2.5-3B and 7B), SGR-OPSD consistently outperforms GRPO and strong self-distillation baselines, improving success rates over GRPO by up to 7.4 percentage points and reaching 85.8% on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-7B.

Ablation studies confirm that subgoal guidance and graph-based weight calibration are key to these performance gains.

A5 Beyond Prompt Injection: The Tool Update Gap in LLM Agent Security

arXiv: 2611.01976 AnthropicGoogleAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Large language model based agents increasingly rely on external tools to invoke services and interact with web resources.

These tools can evolve through software updates, yet agentic frameworks may continue trusting them without re-verifying their safety after implementation changes.

This tool update gap creates a supply-chain vulnerability in agentic systems: because updated tool code executes outside the LLM's context window, malicious functionality embedded in tool updates can bypass defenses centered on model-visible interactions, leaving agents exposed to stealthy, persistent threats that existing in-context defenses can hardly address.

To thoroughly investigate this threat, we introduce ETUDE (Exploiting Tool Updates for Direct Execution), an attack that embeds malicious functionality into updates of initially benign tools to produce compromised tools, while preserving their legitimate tool functionality.

Unlike prompt-based attacks, ETUDE operates entirely outside the LLM's context and requires no adversarial instructions, thereby bypassing defenses restricted to model-visible content.

We evaluate ETUDE across three representative attack workloads and seven widely used commercial and open-source agentic frameworks, including Gemini CLI, Codex CLI, and Claude Code.

The results show that ETUDE achieves a 97.6% ASR while maintaining a 95.9% legitimate task completion rate.

We further examine existing defenses and find that they provide insufficient protection against this threat.

These findings expose a fundamental blind spot in current agent security practices: trust established for a tool does not necessarily remain valid after its implementation updates.

A6 Isomorphism Is Not Enough: An Agentic Audit of Verified Code Generation in Lean

arXiv: 2605.23772 OpenAIAnthropicKimi

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Agentic systems have recently emerged as state-of-the-art approaches for automated theorem proving in formal mathematics.

To assess how far these capabilities extend to program verification and how reliably existing benchmarks can measure them, we audit CLEVER, a Lean 4 benchmark for verifiable code generation, using three independent agentic stacks based on Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, and Kimi K2.5.

Our audit finds arguably erroneous reference specifications in 80 of 161 entries, each accepted by the benchmark's authors, distills a taxonomy of benchmark errors, and exposes structural limitations of isomorphism-based scoring, whose signal breaks down when generated and reference specifications formalize different yet defensible readings.

Looking ahead, we translate our findings into recommendations for future work, with our error analysis doubling as a repair list for CLEVER and a heuristic checklist for the next generation of benchmarks.

A8 NeMo-DCR: Bit-Exact Delta-Compressed Refit for Scalable Agentic RL at Trillion-Parameter Scale

arXiv: 2610.08430 KimiAgentic RLAgent

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) disaggregates training from rollout into different clusters, so each policy update must reach rollout clusters before the next batch: a full 1T checkpoint takes 87.5 min between two AWS regions, yet only 1-2% of BF16 elements change their stored values per step.

Concurrent systems use this sparsity but reimplement placement rules, assemble full tensors, rebuild values arithmetically, or rely on a cross-cluster collective, and none fully recovers from mid-refit failures.

We present NeMo-DCR (Delta-Compressed Refit), which sends only changes yet gives receivers the bits of a dense refit.

NeMo-DCR describes changes in the Hugging Face canonical coordinates that both sides already share and leaves their placement to the serving runtime's native loader.

It projects over 96% of MoE weight bytes directly and converts the rest, under 4%.

It encodes changes as compressible XOR masks for over 96% of these bytes and as overwrites where XOR masks cannot apply, streams them over object storage or a relay tree without a cross-cluster collective, and updates receivers in place, recovering from failures with overwrite retries and a joint commit.

With real updates, NeMo-DCR is $7\times$ faster than AReaL-DTE.

In 3% and 5% stress cases, 30B-1T refits are 12-40$\times$ faster than a transport-only full-checkpoint reference over the same object storage, and would make a synchronous Kimi K2 RL step 1.4-1.5$\times$ faster at 1T.

A11 Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models

arXiv: 2610.23344 QwenRSIWorld Model

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate AI research, including language-model (LM) development, offering a potential path toward recursive self-improvement.

However, turning experimental feedback into a learned ability to propose effective improvements across models faces two challenges.

First, training such an ability with real feedback is expensive: reinforcement learning (RL) requires evaluating tens of thousands of proposals, each potentially requiring a full model-training run.

Even at ten GPU-hours per run, reward collection alone would consume hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours, motivating a cheaper surrogate.

Second, surrogate feedback can become unreliable as policy optimization favors overestimated proposals when the proposal distribution shifts.

To address these challenges, we propose LM2LM, which trains LLMs with RL to generate effective LM improvement proposals.

To provide scalable feedback, we use a research world model (RWM), a frozen LLM that predicts proposal gains from real experimental records provided in context, as the reward model for RL.

To maintain feedback reliability, we periodically execute current-policy proposals ranked highly by the RWM and add their measured outcomes to its context.

Across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments, real-execution evaluations on held-out tasks show that LM2LM enables Qwen3.8-27B to outperform GPT-6-sol, even when GPT-6-sol is given 300 real experimental records in context, while requiring 33% lower data-generation cost.

LM2LM also generalizes to a model family unseen during training.

A12 DR Pixel: Page-Native Deep Research with Vision-Language Models

arXiv: 2611.03097 OpenAIGoogleQwen

📝 中文摘要（中英对照）：

We instead ask whether vision-language models (VLMs) can enable page-native deep research: directly retrieving and reasoning over rendered document page images.

We introduce DR Pixel, the first open-source end-to-end multimodal DR agent that uses rendered page images as its sole retrieval and reasoning unit, operating over a corpus of 1.06M pages.

To evaluate this setting, we construct DRPixelBench, a benchmark on scientific papers requiring reasoning over text, figures, tables, and equations.
